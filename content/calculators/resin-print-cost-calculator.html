Why resin prints cost more than the resin

Filament printing has one consumable. Resin printing has four, and only one of them shows up in the slicer. The calculator adds them together:

resin = ml used × (1 + waste%) × bottle price ÷ bottle volume
washing = IPA replaced per print (ml) ÷ 1000 × price per liter
FEP wear = sheet cost ÷ prints per sheet
electricity = watts ÷ 1000 × hours × price per kWh
total = resin + washing + FEP wear + electricity + consumables

Worked example

A plate of four 25 ml miniatures (100 ml total) in $30/L resin, three hours on a 60 W printer.

  • Resin: 100 ml × 1.10 × $0.03 = $3.30
  • Washing: 30 ml IPA at $8/L = $0.24
  • FEP: $8 ÷ 60 prints = $0.13
  • Electricity: 0.06 kW × 3 h × $0.15 = $0.03
  • Consumables: $0.15

Total $3.85, or about $0.96 per miniature. Resin is 86% of it — but if you'd quoted on the slicer's 100 ml alone you'd have said $3.00 and been 28% under.

Getting the inputs right

Waste

Resin that clings to the model and supports and washes off in the IPA is the biggest item — a heavily supported miniature can carry 10% of its own volume in uncured resin. Add what's left in the vat when you pour back through a filter, the occasional print stuck to the FEP, and a failure now and then. 10% is honest for a careful workflow; 15–20% if you're still learning.

IPA

The bucket volume is irrelevant; what matters is how much you replace and how often. If you dump 500 ml every 15 prints, enter 33 ml. Two-stage washing (dirty then clean) stretches alcohol a long way. If you use water-washable resin, set the IPA line to a few cents for water and disposal — but note water-washable resin usually costs more per liter.

FEP film

A standard FEP sheet lasts 20 to 100+ prints depending on how well it's treated. Big flat plates, heavy supports and failures shorten it; nFEP and PFA release films often go longer. If you replace the film every couple of months, count the prints between changes.

Power

MSLA printers are frugal: 40–80 W is typical for a 4K–14K desktop machine, with larger 10-inch-plus printers around 80–120 W. Electricity ends up being a few cents per print. The curing station and wash station are similarly small; fold them into consumables unless you run them for long stretches.

From cost to price

This page costs the print. To turn it into a selling price — machine depreciation, your time cleaning and curing, a failure allowance, markup and marketplace fees — take the total into the 3D print pricing calculator and enter it as material cost. Resin post-processing is labor-intensive, so be honest about the minutes: washing, support removal, curing and the inevitable sanding of support nubs is rarely under ten minutes a plate.
