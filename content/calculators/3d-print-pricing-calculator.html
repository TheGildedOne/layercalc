What a 3D print actually costs

Most people price prints by filament alone, or by a rule like "three times the material". Both undercharge, because on any print longer than an hour the plastic is the cheapest thing involved. The calculator builds the price from five real costs and one honest allowance:

material = grams ÷ 1000 × price per kg
machine = hours × (printer cost ÷ life hours + maintenance per hour)
electricity = watts ÷ 1000 × hours × price per kWh
labor = minutes ÷ 60 × your hourly rate
failures = (material + machine + electricity) × (1 ÷ (1 − failure rate) − 1)
cost = all of the above + other per-item costs
price = cost × (1 + markup)

Failures are applied only to the machine-side costs. A failed print wastes plastic, machine hours and power; it doesn't usually double the packing time.

Worked example

A 60 g PLA print that takes 6 hours on a $400 printer, sold on Etsy.

  • Material: 60 g × $0.022 = $1.32
  • Depreciation: 6 h × $400 ÷ 3,000 h = $0.80
  • Maintenance: 6 h × $0.05 = $0.30
  • Electricity: 0.12 kW × 6 h × $0.15 = $0.11
  • Labor: 15 min at $20/h = $5.00
  • Packaging: $0.50
  • Failure allowance at 5%: ($1.32 + $0.80 + $0.30 + $0.11) × 0.0526 = $0.13

Total cost $8.16. With a 50% markup the price is $12.24. To net that on Etsy, list at ($12.24 + $0.45) ÷ 0.905 = $14.02. Notice that "three times material" would have given $3.96 — a loss on the labor alone.

Reading the numbers straight out of your G-code

Every mainstream slicer writes the figures it calculated into comments at the top or bottom of the file. Paste that block into the box above and the filament weight and print time are filled in for you, which removes the most common source of error in a quote — transcribing the wrong number, or last week's number.

PrusaSlicer / OrcaSlicer / Bambu Studio
  ; filament used [g] = 61.2
  ; estimated printing time (normal mode) = 6h 12m 30s

Cura
  ;Filament used: 20.4m
  ;TIME:22350

Open the .gcode in any text editor and copy the first forty lines or so. Parsing happens in your browser and the file never leaves your machine — you can paste the whole file if it is small, though the header alone is enough.

Choosing the inputs

Printer life

A hobby machine run carefully lasts several thousand print hours before it's worth more as parts than as a printer. 3,000 hours is a fair middle figure; use 2,000 for a budget bedslinger you push hard and 5,000 for a well-built CoreXY you maintain. The number matters less than you'd think — halving it adds about 13 cents an hour on a $400 printer.

Maintenance per hour

Nozzles, PTFE tubes, belts, build plates, fans and the occasional hotend add up to roughly $100–200 a year for a printer in regular use. Divide that by the hours it runs: at 2,000 hours a year that's $0.05–0.10/h. Abrasive filaments (glow, carbon fiber) chew nozzles much faster; budget accordingly.

Power draw

Use an average, not the power supply rating. A typical bedslinger printing PLA averages 80–120 W after warm-up; enclosed printers running ABS at 100 °C bed temperatures average 150–250 W. The electricity calculator has presets, or measure with a plug-in meter for a real figure.

Labor

Count everything you'd otherwise not be doing: slicing, starting the print, removing it, cleaning supports, sanding, packing, replying to the buyer. Fifteen minutes is a fair minimum for a simple part; anything painted or assembled is far more. Charge yourself a real rate — if you wouldn't do it for that money, the price is wrong.

Markup vs. margin

Markup is added to cost; margin is the share of the sale price that's profit. They describe the same money differently: a 50% markup is a 33% margin, a 100% markup is a 50% margin. The calculator shows both. Commodity parts that anyone with a printer could make support 30–50%; custom, designed or finished work supports 100% and up.

Marketplace fees

Fees are charged on the listed price, including shipping in most cases, which is why you can't just add them on afterwards. If you want to keep P after a percentage fee f and a fixed fee c:

list price = (P + c) ÷ (1 − f)

The Etsy preset uses 6.5% transaction + 3% payment processing and $0.25 + $0.20 listing per sale (about 9.5% and $0.45); eBay uses roughly 13.6% and $0.30. Both marketplaces change their schedules, and processing fees vary by country, so check the current rates and edit the fields if yours differ. Etsy's Offsite Ads fee (12–15%, only on attributed sales) isn't included; if a large share of your sales come through it, bump the percentage.

What this doesn't include

Design time, which should be billed separately or spread across expected sales; taxes; shipping, which you pass through at cost; and the cost of your printer sitting idle, which is real but not the customer's problem. If the price the calculator gives is higher than what similar things sell for, that's information: either your costs are higher than the competition's, or theirs are being ignored.
