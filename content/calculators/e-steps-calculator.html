The test, step by step


    
  
  1. Heat the hotend to your normal printing temperature so filament can actually flow. Leave the nozzle over empty air or above the bed.
    2. 
  
  2. Using calipers, mark the filament above the point it enters the extruder (the top of the extruder body, or the end of the Bowden coupler). Marlin convention is 120 mm; Klipper's documentation specifies 70 mm. A second mark 10 mm lower helps if the first gets pulled inside.
    3. 
  
  3. Set relative extrusion and push slowly. Marlin: M83 then G1 E100 F100. Klipper: G91 then G1 E50 F60, which is what its own docs specify. The slow feed rate matters more than the distance — Klipper's documentation warns that a faster rate builds pressure in the extruder and skews the result, and cautions against using the interface's extrude buttons because they run faster. Fast extrusion tests under-read because the melt can't keep up.
    4. 
  
  4. Measure from the extruder entry to the mark. Ideally 20 mm remains. If more remains, the extruder pushed less than asked (under-extrusion); if less, it pushed more.
    5. 
  
  5. Enter your current value and the measurements above, save the new value with the commands shown, and run the test again. A second pass within ±0.5 mm is as good as it gets. Klipper's docs suggest repeating the measurement outright if the first miss is more than about 2 mm, and the calculator flags that case.
    6. 




The formulas


Marlin:   new e-steps = current × requested ÷ actual
Klipper:  new rotation_distance = current × actual ÷ requested


The Klipper line is stated the same way in its own documentation, which also asks for the result rounded to three decimal places — so that is what this page outputs when Klipper is selected.


They're inverses because the two firmwares describe the extruder in opposite units. Marlin counts steps per millimeter of filament, so under-extrusion means more steps are needed. Klipper describes millimeters per motor revolution, so under-extrusion means each revolution moved less than the config assumed, and the number comes down. Mixing them up moves you twice as far from correct — the calculator won't let you.





Worked example


An Ender 3 at the stock 93 steps/mm. You mark at 120 mm, extrude 100, and 23 mm remains, so only 97 mm went in.


    
  
  • Marlin: 93 × 100 ÷ 97 = 95.88 → M92 E95.88, M500
    • 
  
  • The same extruder on Klipper with rotation_distance: 33.5: 33.5 × 97 ÷ 100 = 32.495
    • 






When the number is far off


A genuine e-steps error is small — a few percent from manufacturing tolerance in the drive gear, or from an upgrade like a dual-gear extruder or a different motor. A result more than 10% away from what you had usually means something else is going on:


    
  
  • Slipping or grinding. The drive gear is skating on the filament: idler tension too low, a partial clog, or the hotend too cold for the speed. Look for a shiny worn spot on the filament.
    • 
  
  • Extruding too fast. 100 mm at F100 is right; at F300 many hotends can't melt fast enough and the reading is meaningless.
    • 
  
  • Measuring from the wrong place. On a Bowden printer measure from the extruder, not the hotend.
    • 
  
  • Absolute extrusion mode. Without M83, G1 E100 means "go to E=100", which may be 0 mm or 100 mm depending on the current E position. Send M83 first, or G92 E0 then the move.
    • 
  
  • A stepper motor with a different step angle (0.9° instead of 1.8°) or a geared extruder swap. These legitimately double or triple the value — the warning still fires, and that's fine if you know why.
    • 




Converting between Marlin and Klipper


A different job from calibration. If you are moving a printer from Marlin to Klipper, you already have a working e-steps figure — you want the equivalent rotation_distance, not a fresh measurement. Klipper documents the conversion:


rotation_distance = full_steps_per_rotation × microsteps ÷ steps_per_mm
steps_per_mm      = full_steps_per_rotation × microsteps ÷ rotation_distance


full_steps_per_rotation is 200 for a 1.8° motor and 400 for a 0.9° one. microsteps is the microstepping your Marlin value was set at; Klipper's documentation says that if you are unsure, use 16 and set microsteps: 16 in the config to match.





Worked example


    
  
  • Stock Ender 3, 93 steps/mm, 16 microsteps: 200 × 16 ÷ 93 = 34.409
    • 
  
  • Same printer with a 0.9° motor: 400 × 16 ÷ 93 = 68.817
    • 






The geared-extruder trap


This is where most conversions go wrong. A Marlin steps/mm figure for a geared extruder — a BMG, a Clockwork, an Orbiter — already has the gearing baked into it. Klipper gives you two ways to express the same extruder:


    
  
  • Without gear_ratio: rotation_distance is measured at the motor. A BMG's 415 steps/mm converts directly to 7.711.
    • 
  
  • With gear_ratio: 50:17: Klipper measures rotation_distance at the final gear instead, so the same extruder needs 22.679 — the direct figure multiplied by the ratio.
    • 



Both are correct. What breaks is mixing them: converting directly and setting gear_ratio leaves you out by the ratio, roughly 3× for a BMG, which shows up as severe over-extrusion on the first print. Tick the gear-ratio box above only if you intend to put gear_ratio in your config.


A converted value is the figure your printer was designed around, not a calibrated one — if the Marlin value was never measured, neither is this. Run the extrusion test at the top of the page afterwards.

What e-steps don't fix


E-steps make the extruder deliver the right length of filament. They don't correct for filament diameter variation, for how the plastic spreads at a given temperature, or for the slicer's assumptions about line width. That's the job of the flow rate, tuned per material after e-steps are set. Do this first, once per extruder; then use the flow rate calculator for each filament; then check dimensions with the shrinkage calculator. Doing them in another order means re-doing them.


Note that the popular Bambu Lab, Prusa MK4 and most Klipper-based printers ship with the extruder already calibrated and tuning flow is all that's normally needed. E-steps matter most on printers that have been modified — a new extruder, a new motor, a new mainboard — or on budget machines where the stock number was never right.
