How it works

filament left (g) = spool weight now − empty spool weight
length left (m) = grams ÷ (π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × density)

Weight is the most direct way to know what's on a spool, because you can't see how many layers are wound underneath the outer one. The single piece of information you need beyond a scale is the empty spool's weight, and that's the one most people don't have — which is why the presets exist.

What about printers that estimate it for you?

Some do, and it is worth knowing how. Bambu's AMS reads the RFID tag on a Bambu spool as it passes the reader once per revolution, and compares that against the length of filament its odometer says it has fed. Length per revolution gives the effective circumference, which gives the spool's current diameter, which maps to the amount remaining — a full roll feeds far more filament per turn than a nearly empty one. A standard 1 kg roll is treated as 100% unless it is told otherwise.

It is a genuinely clever approach and it works. But it is geometry-derived rather than a measurement of mass, so it can drift; it assumes a standard roll; and it depends on readable RFID tags. Third-party spools, re-spooled filament and anything without a tag fall back to a figure you set by hand. Weighing sidesteps every one of those assumptions, which is why a scale stays useful even on a printer that estimates.

Worked example

A Hatchbox PLA spool reads 640 g on the kitchen scale.

  • Filament left: 640 − 225 = 415 g (about 41% of the original kilo)
  • Length: 415 ÷ 2.98 g/m = 139 m
  • A print needing 380 g will make it with 35 g to spare — enough, but a mid-print runout would waste 380 g, so it's worth double-checking the tare on a reel you've weighed yourself.

A habit that pays off: when a spool runs out, weigh it, and write the number on the box or in a note. After a few months you'll have exact tares for every brand you buy, and the presets become unnecessary.

Turning grams into prints

Your slicer reports the grams a print needs. Compare directly with what's left, and keep a margin — the tare uncertainty alone can be ±15 g, and printers waste a little on purge lines and retractions the slicer doesn't count. If the margin is under about 10% of the print's weight, it's a gamble; either use a fresh spool, or plan to swap spools mid-print (most modern printers pause on runout, and most filaments can be spliced or simply changed at a pause).
