8 SEPTEMBER - REDDIT FEEDBACK
---------------------------------------------------------------------

8a03c62  Acted on the first community feedback
  Every claim verified against primary sources before changing
  anything. Three were genuine errors:

  * FEP wear was counted per print. It is per peel cycle, and one
    peel is one layer - so a 2,000-layer print wears film about ten
    times as much as a 200-layer one. On the worked example this
    moved film from $0.13 to $2.67, making it the second-largest
    cost in the job.
  * The Bambu AMS description was wrong. It does not simply track
    from a full spool; it divides filament length fed by RFID-tag
    revolutions to infer the spool's current diameter.
  * The layer height page implied magic numbers always help. Prusa's
    own forum calls the concept "largely for early 3D printers with
    old design Stepper Drivers" and "not a big issue with Prusa
    Printers".

  Resin calculator also gained ml/L/g/kg input with density
  conversion, configurable IPA use, and optional LCD amortisation.

  tests/ created - a dependency-free DOM shim that runs each built
  page's real script and asserts on rendered output. 101 checks,
  wired into CI so a wrong formula fails the deploy.

8 SEPTEMBER - COMPETITOR RESEARCH PASSES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

6b44a75  Adopted documented procedures, borrowed two features
  * Flow rate: Ellis' Print Tuning Guide lists "tuning extrusion
    multiplier by measuring 1-2 cube walls" as its FIRST
    misconception - which was exactly and only our method. The page
    now computes, live from your own numbers, how much a 0.02 mm
    calliper error moves the result (4.2% on a 0.48 mm wall), and
    points at the top-surface method instead.
  * E-steps: Klipper documents its own procedure - 70 mm mark,
    50 mm extruded at F60, three decimals, repeat if off by >2 mm.
    We were applying Marlin's convention throughout. Selecting
    Klipper now switches to its documented figures.
  * Prints per spool (from SpoolMath / CrunchMilk), waste- and
    failure-adjusted: a 1 kg spool and a 50 g print is 18, not 20.
  * G-code header parsing (from calc3dprint). Reads filament weight
    and print time from a pasted slicer header - PrusaSlicer, Orca,
    Bambu and Cura formats - straight into the pricing calculator.

80b5969  Layer height calculator rebuilt
  Checked printpal.io and grandpacad directly: both take a lead screw
  and a motor angle and return a list of multiples. Nothing else.
  Reframed the page to answer "what layer height should I use?":
  * Stair-stepping - cusp height and step width at any slope angle,
    plus an inverse solve for a target smoothness. A vertical wall
    has zero deviation at any layer height; a 15 deg slope at 0.2 mm
    steps 0.75 mm and looks terraced.
  * Flow-aware relative print time.
  * Feature fit - 5 mm at 0.3 mm layers prints as 4.8 or 5.1.
  * Magic numbers kept but demoted, with a verdict conditioned on
    driver type and mesh settings.

a97dde1  Remaining calculators - one was actively wrong
  * SHRINKAGE BUG. OrcaSlicer's field takes the MEASURED percentage
    (99.45), not the scale factor (100.55). They are reciprocals, so
    following our advice scaled the part the wrong way and roughly
    doubled the error. Now a slicer selector gives the one right
    value for the one right field.
  * Volumetric speed: generates Ellis' extrude-only test commands
    for your filament diameter, plus a safety margin because bench
    figures read high.
  * Belt tension: CoreXY belt matching, and a note that the
    vibrating-string model ignores bending stiffness so short spans
    read high.
  * Model scale: bed fit now evaluated in every mode (it was gated
    to the one mode where the scale is chosen to fit, so it could
    never fail), diagonal placement, and layer quantisation.
  * Print time: reads the slicer's own figures from a pasted header.

0eb59aa  Electricity and filament calculators
  I had called these "just unit conversions". Each was assuming an
  input was exact when it is not:
  * Electricity: warm-up modelled separately from steady state. On
    an 8-hour print heating is 2% of the energy; on a 15-minute
    print it is 50%. Idle draw added - 8 W left on 24/7 costs
    $10.51/yr, over half what the printing itself costs.
  * Length/weight: weight scales with diameter SQUARED, so a
    +/-0.02 mm spool tolerance is +/-2.3% on 1.75 mm - larger than
    the gap between several material densities. Now shown, and a
    measured diameter can override the nominal.
  * Filament remaining: a geometric method for spools you cannot
    take off the printer. Taking the annulus ratio against a full
    spool cancels the packing factor, which is the reason naive
    geometric methods are unreliable.

d6ec206  Tower generator - three more tests
  Orca's built-in calibration covers temperature, flow, pressure
  advance, retraction, tolerance and volumetric speed. Two genuine
  gaps in every mainstream slicer, now generated here:
  * Cooling fan tower - the main lever on the overhang vs adhesion
    trade, and material-specific. Nothing ships a test for it.
  * Print speed tower.
  Plus pressure advance (Klipper SET_PRESSURE_ADVANCE / Marlin
  M900 K), with an honest note that a dedicated PA pattern reads
  better than a tower.
  Also stopped asserting our calibration order as the only one -
  Orca's guide differs, both are defensible, and the pages say so.

24ea20e  SEO audit of the site itself
  * Heading hierarchy: card grids are h3 and sat directly under the
    h1 on category pages and the 404, skipping a level. Fixed.
  * Sitemap lastmod was stamping the build date on hub pages, so
    every deploy claimed they had changed. A lastmod that always
    moves teaches crawlers to ignore it. Hubs now inherit the newest
    date of the content they list; a no-op rebuild produces a
    byte-identical sitemap.
  * Authorship added to structured data (author, publisher,
    datePublished, dateModified).
  * docs/SEO.md records what is deliberately NOT done and why.

  Two findings worth remembering: FAQ rich results were deprecated
  in May 2026 (schema still worth keeping for AI Overviews, but it
  produces no expanded listing), and llms.txt is close to useless -
  most such files are never requested and Google has said it does
  not support the format.

WHERE THINGS STAND
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  25 pages live, all returning 200
  201 calculator tests passing, wired into CI
  1,255 build-time validation checks
  ~6 KB per page gzipped
  Zero broken links, zero orphans
  HTTPS enforced, redirects correct, indexed by Google and Bing

  Bugs found and fixed across the day: 3 genuine errors, one of
  which (the Orca shrinkage reciprocal) was actively making users'
  prints worse.

  The recurring lesson: the arithmetic was almost never wrong. What
  was wrong was an input silently assumed exact, a slicer field
  whose semantics we had not checked, or a position stated more
  firmly than the sources justified.
